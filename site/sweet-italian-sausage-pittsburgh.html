What Makes It Ours

"Sweet" Just Means Mild.

In Italian sausage, sweet is the traditional name for the version without chili heat. Ours isn't a stripped-down version of the hot: it's the Abruzzo-style blend the Ricci family brought over from Sulmona, seasoned with cracked black pepper and no fennel or garlic at all. Hand-mixed in 200-pound batches, natural casing, 22–25% fat so it stays juicy on the grill. All natural, USDA-inspected every day since 1973.

It's the best seller in the case for a reason: it works everywhere. Grilled at a tailgate, browned into Sunday gravy, sliced over rigatoni, or crumbled into stuffing at Thanksgiving. If someone in Pittsburgh tells you their grandmother's sauce recipe calls for "the sausage from Ricci's" — this is the one.

Questions

FAQ

Is sweet Italian sausage actually sweet?

It doesn't taste sweet — "sweet" is just the traditional name for the version without chili heat. It's our Abruzzo-style original: cracked black pepper, no fennel, no garlic, and none of the paprika or crushed red pepper that make the hot sausage hot.

Where can I buy it in Pittsburgh?

At our McKees Rocks shop, 500 Pine Hollow Road — ten minutes from downtown Pittsburgh. Or shipped frozen anywhere in the U.S. in the Pittsburgh Italian Pack.

What's the best way to cook it?

Stovetop with a splash of water, on the grill over medium heat, or browned and simmered in Sunday gravy. It also crumbles beautifully for pasta, pizza, and stuffing.
