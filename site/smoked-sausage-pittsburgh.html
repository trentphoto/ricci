Fresh vs. Smoked

Why Fresh Sausage Wins.

Smoked sausage — kielbasa, andouille, the ring sausages you grill straight from the package — is cooked and preserved before you ever buy it. It's good at what it does. But it's a different food from what an Italian family in Pittsburgh means by sausage.

Fresh Italian sausage is raw pork, salt, whole fennel seed, and garlic in a natural casing — nothing cooked, nothing cured, nothing preserved. The flavor develops in your pan, on your grill, in your Sunday gravy. That's how the Ricci family made it in Sulmona, Italy, and how we've made it on Pine Hollow Road since 1945: 200-pound hand-mixed batches, real paprika instead of extract, fennel seed we refuse to buy ground, and no MSG, fillers, or preservatives.

And if it's smoke flavor you're after — our Sausage Club ships a smoke-paprika seasonal cut every October.

FAQ

Do you sell smoked sausage?

Not as a regular item — everything in the case is fresh. The one exception: Sausage Club members get a smoke-paprika seasonal cut every October.

What's the difference between fresh and smoked sausage?

Smoked sausage is cooked and preserved before sale; fresh sausage is raw and you cook it yourself. Fresh gives you the flavor of the meat and spices, not the preservation method.

How do I cook fresh Italian sausage?

Stovetop with a splash of water, on the grill to 160°F internal, or browned and dropped into Sunday gravy for 90 minutes. Full instructions ship with every order.

Do you ship outside Pittsburgh?

Nationwide — vacuum-sealed and frozen, packed in an insulated box, shipped Monday–Wednesday. See shipping info.
