LANDING PAGE DESIGN AND CRO PRINCIPLES

The four versions test different ways of helping someone feel ready to order.
They share the same offer, product choices, and now the same core proof and
food photography.

ORIGINAL

Design approach: A thorough sales page covering the offer, contents, process,
value, reviews, guarantee, shipping, and story.

CRO hypothesis: Answer enough objections that an unfamiliar visitor becomes
comfortable buying. Its strength is reassurance; its risk is length.

MINIMAL

Design approach: Centered white layout, narrow reading width, generous
spacing, and restrained typography.

CRO hypothesis: Reduce distraction and make the offer easy to understand.
Product photos and proof support the decision without competing for attention.
Its risk is feeling less distinctive or persuasive.

BUTCHERBOX-INSPIRED

Design approach: Familiar food-commerce structure: large food hero, benefits
strip, product cards, simple steps, then ordering.

CRO hypothesis: Make an unfamiliar brand feel easy to shop. Visitors can
quickly understand what they get and how it works. Its risk is looking like
other meat-delivery brands.

TABLE / CUSTOM

Design approach: Warm editorial styling, emotional headline, prominent food
photography, and a complete order panel beside the hero on desktop.

CRO hypothesis: Combine appetite and the feeling of gathering with family
with an immediate, practical buying decision. Its risk is that the emotional
opening won't resonate with everyone.

SHARED CONVERSION PRINCIPLES

1. Make the offer explicit: $189, shipping included, one-time purchase.
   Those details sit close to the buying decision.

2. Keep the decision small: three blend choices, one price, one checkout
   destination.

3. Use photos for two jobs: cooked food creates appetite; raw coils and the
   contents section explain what actually arrives.

4. Put proof behind the promise: customer reviews provide personal
   reassurance; the ESPN feature adds recognizable credibility.

5. Help people picture using it: the grill, gravy, and sandwich sections turn
   a large quantity of sausage into familiar meals.

6. Keep ordering accessible: repeated Buy buttons, plus mobile sticky buttons
   on Butcher and Table.

The strongest hypothesis for Table is that desire and clarity work best
together: "I want that meal" and "I understand exactly what I'm buying."

These are hypotheses, not established winners. Because we're measuring Buy
clicks, the experiment will tell us which presentation gets more people to
checkout—not necessarily which produces more sales.
